# Provider behavior and audit decisions

Reviewed against current and legacy official documentation in November 2026. Documentation defines expected semantics; populated account evidence establishes the actual response and effective behavior. This reference supplies reasons for the [check catalog](oncall-checks.md), not universal customer thresholds or permission to change configuration.

| Outcome | Documented behavior that affects the audit | Decision and retest |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reach the intended responder | Escalation delays accumulate. An unavailable earlier target does not imply that later waits are skipped. Nested policies and adjacent rotation targets are distinct actions. | Preserve ordered paths and compare total configured delay with the declared objective. Re-read every referenced policy/roster; a direct email/webhook target is not a proven enrolled human. |
| Apply the effective notification policy | Primary/custom selection and time restrictions matter. Global paging can supersede personal settings. Creating the first global policy can enforce it for all users; large organizations may take time to propagate. | Read the effective selection, verified contacts and post-propagation state. Do not credit unused personal settings or enable global enforcement as a harmless test. |
| Suppress only intended work | Maintenance can cover multiple overlapping route sets. An empty route selection is global; existing paging may continue, and ending maintenance can release pending work. The documented API does not supply automatic expiry. | Identify exact matching instances, owner, intended end and restoration. Block unknown intent; fail a demonstrated conflict with required paging. Verify affected and unaffected routes. |
| Preserve identity and routing | Transformation order, global/route matching and stop behavior affect the final event. The rules engine uses provider matching semantics; a Python regex approximation is insufficient. | Inspect the whole pipeline and paired positive/negative observations. Preserve stable condition identity, intended severity and routing; do not hide critical alerts to lower counts. |
| Distinguish noise from processing | Incident deduplication follows entity identity while the incident is unresolved. Notification aggregation is separate from deduplication. A missing entity identity or unsupported message state can change behavior. | Compare source trigger/recovery identity with incident episodes. Do not call incident counts, alert totals and device notifications the same measure. Missing source lifecycle remains unknown. |
| Measure useful response | Reporting filters default to resolved incidents. Transition evidence and explicitly bounded all-phase cohorts are necessary for response analysis. | Include open/censored cases and sample counts; observed-subset latency is not population MTTA/MTTR. Response objectives and paging budgets come from the operator. |
| Understand workload | On-call review reports duty time, can lag and does not count notification interruptions. Assignment and duty identifiers need an evidenced join. | Compare only against declared staffing/business hours. Unequal counts do not prove unfairness; show missing channel/interruption evidence. |
| Protect future coverage | Schedules have timezones, partial-day masks, boundaries and overrides. An unassigned absence can leave its original responder on duty. | Assess a declared interval, including adjacent and relevant DST boundaries. Current roster, periods or an initial schedule row alone cannot certify continuous coverage. |
| Preserve critical-source continuity | Auto-pause/high-lag and auto-resolution/acknowledgement-expiry are separate controls from source recovery. Source throttling, grouping and `send_resolved` have their own semantics. | Inspect sender health, loaded routing and provider control state. Never flood a provider to test its documented threshold or equate administrative resolution with recovery. |
| Provide actionable context | Entity, service, environment, owner, symptoms and runbook context must describe the original trigger. A later recovery alert may contain different fields. | Inspect the original alert safely; field presence is not correctness, ownership or a reachable runbook. Keep application IDs source-verified. |

## External comparison, with limits

[Google SRE monitoring guidance](https://sre.google/sre-book/monitoring-distributed-systems/) motivates actionable, urgent, user-impacting pages and careful noise reduction. It does not supply a provider-specific default failure threshold or ground truth for precision/recall. The [official Terraform provider](https://github.com/splunk/terraform-provider-victorops) and [Grafana migration tooling](https://github.com/grafana/irm-tooling) expose useful compatibility cases: rate limits, resource replacement, rounded delays and unsupported shapes. Their workarounds are historical implementation evidence, not instructions to delete/recreate customer resources or weaken this plugin's preservation guards.

Review source monitoring separately: grouping, inhibition, debounce, severity, delivery/recovery configuration, data continuity and service identity belong to the source audit/setup. On-Call owns destination routing, effective paging, responders and incident handling configuration. Both evidence planes must agree before an end-to-end claim. Seat counts or subscriptions alone do not establish waste; any cost recommendation requires an actual invoice/contract and agreed staffing needs. Splunk platform pricing/telemetry and a future backend integration are distinct capabilities.
